## Lead Story: Court Orders Production of Training Documents for GenAI Review

A New York federal court granted plaintiff's motion to compel a random sample of training documents used for defendant's GenAI review system.

### What Happened

Judge Nelson S. Roman (S.D.N.Y.) granted plaintiff's request for:
- A random sample of documents used to train the GenAI system
- Documents showing how the system learned from human review decisions
- Sufficient to assess accuracy and reliability of AI-assisted coding

### The Significance

This extends TAR precedent into GenAI workflows:
- Traditional TAR: courts have ordered disclosure of seed set documents, protocols, and validation statistics
- GenAI review: same transparency principles now applying to AI training data
- The court found plaintiff's need to assess AI review reliability outweighs defendant's confidentiality concerns

### What This Means for Practice

1. **Document your training**: GenAI systems that learn from human review create a discoverable record
2. **Expect transparency requests**: Opposing counsel may request training samples, not just protocols
3. **Protect confidential data**: Use protective orders to limit use of training documents to the litigation
4. **Validate before production**: Document accuracy metrics before discovery disputes arise

### The Precedent Path

Courts have been moving this direction for traditional TAR:
- *In re: B.P. oil spill litigation*: ordered disclosure of TAR protocols and seed documents
- *Hildebrand v. Saint Louis Heart Center*: granted request for TAR methodology and validation
- *Gordon v. T.G. Iowa, Inc.*: ordered disclosure of TAR process documentation

GenAI review now faces the same scrutiny—with training data as the new seed set.

## Double Standard in AI Protective Orders?

Mike Litan raises a question worth examining: are protective orders being applied differently when plaintiffs use AI versus when defendants use AI?

### The Question

- Plaintiffs using AI tools to analyze produced documents: protective orders often don't restrict
- Defendants using AI for review before production: protective orders increasingly being sought
- Are parties being asked to disclose AI use differently depending on their role in litigation?

### The Concern

Litan notes:
- Protective orders typically limit what receiving parties can do with produced documents
- But some parties are seeking orders that restrict producing parties' use of AI in review
- This creates asymmetric obligations: defendants may face restrictions plaintiffs don't

### The Analogy

Consider traditional document review:
- Producing party reviews documents for privilege before production
- Receiving party can use any method to analyze produced documents
- No protective order restricts the producing party's review methodology

With AI:
- Should producing parties face restrictions on AI review tools?
- Should receiving parties face restrictions on AI analysis tools?
- Or should the same rules apply regardless of AI use?

### Practical Implications

1. **Review your protective order template**: Does it address AI use asymmetrically?
2. **Consider bilateral restrictions**: If one side faces AI limits, should both?
3. **Document your AI use**: Transparency may reduce disputes over methodology
4. **Negotiate early**: AI provisions in protective orders are becoming standard meet-and-confer topics

## Open Weights, Open Questions: The AI Policy Fight

ComplexDiscovery covers the letter from 26 organizations opposing limits on open model weights.

### What Happened

On July 23, 2026, a coalition of 26 organizations sent a letter to the U.S. Senate opposing legislation that would restrict access to open-weights AI models:
- Open-weight models: publicly available model parameters that can be downloaded and modified
- The coalition argues restrictions would harm innovation, research, and security
- The letter responds to policy proposals to limit AI model access for safety reasons

### The Arguments

**For open weights:**
- Enables security research and vulnerability discovery
- Allows academic research on AI safety
- Prevents concentration of AI capability in large companies
- Enables competition and innovation

**For restrictions:**
- Reduces risk of misuse by bad actors
- Limits access to powerful capabilities
- Concentrates responsibility in accountable organizations

### The Legal Tech Implications

Open-weight models affect eDiscovery:
- Tools built on open models can be customized for specific use cases
- Privacy: data stays local when using downloaded models
- But: open models may not match commercial model quality
- Compliance: organizations need governance frameworks for any AI use

### What to Watch

- Congressional action on AI model access legislation
- EU AI Act implementation affecting model weights in Europe
- State-level regulations on AI capability access
- Litigation over AI tool governance in discovery
