## Lead Story: Non-Responsive Family Members Must Be Produced

Delaware Special Master Monté T. Squire granted plaintiffs' motion to compel production of all non-responsive, non-privileged family members of responsive documents in *GlaxoSmithKline Biologicals SA v. Pfizer, Inc.*

### What Happened

- Defendants withheld more than 110,000 allegedly non-responsive attached and embedded files
- They substituted slipsheets in productions instead of producing family members
- Plaintiffs moved to compel, citing identical ESI Order language Judge Williams had already interpreted in related Moderna cases

### The Ruling

Special Master Squire granted the motion, finding:

> "The Court's concern that withholding non-responsive family members could render responsive documents 'incomprehensible, or nearly incomprehensible, and therefore useless' is not case-specific and applies to the instant dispute."

Key holdings:
1. **Judge Williams' prior ruling controls**: The consolidated opinion was docketed in this case, putting defendants on "unequivocal notice"
2. **Context is categorical**: The rationale applies to all document families, not document-by-document
3. **Burge of proof stays with producing party**: Requiring receiving party to demonstrate incomprehensibility for each of 110,000+ documents "would effectively shift PBNT's discovery obligations to GSK"
4. **Burge is self-inflicted**: "Had PBNT complied from the outset... PBNT would not now face a compressed timeline"

### ESI Order Language

Paragraph 5(h) provided:
- Parent-child relationships must be preserved
- Logos, junk files, and blank HTM files may be excluded
- Receiving party may request specific withheld attachments with good-faith basis

### Practical Takeaways

1. **Preserve entire families**: Non-responsive attachments to responsive emails must be produced
2. **Document your process**: ESI Order negotiations create expectations—silence doesn't protect you
3. **Check prior rulings**: Related cases with identical ESI Orders put you on notice
4. **Burden arguments fail**: "Compressed timeline" from non-compliance doesn't excuse production

## Human in the Loop Is Not Enough

Jerry Lawson's LLRX article challenges the comfort phrase that dominates AI governance discussions.

### The Problem

"Human in the loop" may be the most comforting phrase in AI—but the human is more likely to be:
- Answering email
- Running late for lunch
- Reviewing output number 37 of 40

### Four Failure Modes

**1. Automation Bias**
- People trust outputs that look polished, confident, and complete
- 1,812+ documented hallucination cases demonstrate the pattern
- Neat formatting increases believability

**2. Cognitive Overload**
- Billing in six-minute increments makes thorough verification impossible
- Reviewers can't meaningfully evaluate high-volume AI output

**3. Dashboard Illusion**
- Reviewer sees tidy paragraph and "Approve" button
- Doesn't see which sources system ignored, assumptions made, prior steps
- Human is "in the loop" but lacks information for effective review

**4. Speed Asymmetry**
- AI generates outputs faster than humans can evaluate them
- Volume overwhelms verification capacity

### When "Human in the Loop" Becomes "Scapegoat in the Loop"

Lawson's warning:

> "Sometimes 'human in the loop' doesn't function as a safety mechanism. It's more like a liability arrangement. The machine does its work, and the human clicks 'approve.' When that happens, we all know whose name will appear in the disciplinary opinion."

### What Effective Review Requires

- **Time**: Reviewers can't rush through 40 decisions before lunch
- **Visibility**: System must expose sources, assumptions, actions, uncertainties
- **Authority**: Reviewer must be able to stop or reverse without punishment
- **Manageable volume**: No design survives volumes beyond human attention

### The Governance Implication

Independence and incentives matter. When performance grading rewards speed, reviewers have little practical incentive to exercise their theoretical authority to reject.

## Legora Acquires Wexler: Fact Intelligence as Infrastructure

Legora (fka Leya), the Stockholm-founded legal AI platform valued at $5.6 billion, announced acquisition of Wexler—a London startup whose engine extracts and verifies case facts.

### What Wexler Does

- Routes document populations through "gated pipeline"
- Staged checks yield individually sourced, checkable findings
- Captures: actor, statement, audience, date, why it matters
- Design goal: verified fact record, not "summaries or loosely sourced excerpts"
- Claimed capability: handle over 1 million pages per case

### Customer Traction

- Clifford Chance, Goodwin, Herbert Smith Freehills Kramer disputes teams
- Burges Salmon: firmwide deployment extended in May
- Revenue grew 8x year-on-year; net revenue retention above 400%

### The Strategic Significance

Legora is not an eDiscovery vendor—it sells an agentic workspace used by:
- 100,000+ lawyers at 1,500+ law firms
- In-house legal teams in 50+ markets
- $100M+ ARR after passing milestone in April

**Max Junestrand (Legora CEO):**

> "Every dispute and every investigation comes down to the facts, and finding them is often the most manual and expensive part of legal work. As agentic systems take on more of that work, fact queries won't just grow, they'll multiply by orders of magnitude. Wexler is the infrastructure built to handle that scale today."

### The Competitive Implication

Review platforms and case-analysis tools have treated chronology and fact management as destination features. Legora's bet inverts the model:
- If agents do the reading, the fact record becomes the substrate
- Whoever owns the substrate owns the workflow above it

### The Pre-Litigation Claim

The announcement's quietest competitive sentence: Corporate legal teams can run Wexler's engine across contract and communication archives to catch "factual exposure before it becomes a claim."

### Governance Questions

Pre-litigation fact development inside corporate data estate creates questions:
- Security teams: scope access, log queries
- Privacy teams: square ongoing mining with retention schedules and minimization commitments
- Counsel: discipline around analytical output generated before any dispute exists
