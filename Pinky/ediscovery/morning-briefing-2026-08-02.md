## Lead Story: IBM Report Exposes AI Governance Gap

The 2026 Cost of a Data Breach Report from IBM and Ponemon Institute documents a widening gap between AI adoption and AI governance.

### The Headline Numbers

- **Global average breach cost**: $4.99 million (up 12%, highest in 21 editions)
- **US average**: $11.5 million (up ~13%, 2.3x global average)
- **AI-driven attack cost**: $6.04 million ($1 million premium over non-AI attacks)
- **Shadow AI incident cost**: $5.39 million (vs. $4.63 million prior year)

### The Governance Failure

**68% of breached organizations had no AI governance policy in place:**
- 35% reported no policy at all
- 33% had policies "still in development"
- Only 32% had policies actually in place (down from 37%)

### Five of Six Governance Controls Declined

Of the six AI governance controls measured in both years, five lost adoption:

| Control | 2026 | 2025 | Change |
|---------|------|------|--------|
| Strict approval processes | 38% | 45% | -7% |
| AI governance technology | 33% | 39% | -6% |
| Governance frameworks | 33% | 39% | -6% |
| Employee training on AI risks | 30% | 36% | -6% |
| Regular audits for unsanctioned AI | 29% | 34% | -5% |
| Adversarial testing/red teaming | 25% | 22% | +3% |

**Only 19%** reported governance and security teams coordinating.

### Shadow AI: The Expensive Unknown

- **43%** of breaches involved shadow AI (up from 20%)
- **49%** caused data loss or compromise
- **42%** disrupted operations
- **35%** produced reputational damage
- **1 in 5** resulted in regulatory fines

Only 29% ran regular audits for unsanctioned AI—leaving most organizations blind to the problem.

### AI-Driven Attacks Accelerating

- **1 in 4** malicious breaches were AI-driven (56% increase)
- **Deepfake/impersonation**: 45% of AI attacks
- **AI-enabled malware**: 19%
- **AI-generated phishing**: 17%
- **62%** targeted critical infrastructure (financial services, energy)

### The Breach Lifecycle Extended

- **Mean time to identify and contain**: 247 days (up from 241)
- Reversed a five-year decline
- Breaches >200 days averaged $5.65M; <200 days averaged $4.32M

### The Discovery Implication

> "An organization that cannot inventory its AI systems cannot secure them, cannot document conformity for them, and cannot reliably preserve what they generate. Prompts, model outputs and agent logs can constitute business records or discoverable ESI depending on content, retention duty and control. Shadow AI puts them where no data map reaches."

## Case Law: Wilson Aerospace v. Boeing — ESI Protocols Don't Override Rules

Washington Magistrate Judge Michelle L. Peterson issued a significant ruling on custodian obligations.

### The Holding

> "A party therefore cannot justify failing to collect obviously relevant documents on the ground that the relevant employee was not designated as an 'ESI custodian' or that such documents might fall outside an agreed search-term workflow."

### Key Principles

1. **ESI protocols implement the Federal Rules—they don't contract around them**
2. **Discovery requests govern scope**, not search terms
3. **Parties remain obligated to conduct "reasonably diligent searches"** of relevant custodians and shared platforms
4. **Search terms don't supplant discovery requests**

### The Protocol Language

The ESI Protocol itself stated:
- Discovery requests govern the scope of documents and ESI to be produced
- Search terms and similar procedures do not supplant that scope
- Parties must conduct reasonably diligent searches and produce relevant, responsive, non-privileged documents

### The Ruling

Judge Peterson allowed "targeted expansion" to add three custodians:
- Gregory Ffolkes
- Ivan Bush
- Patrick Murphy

Plus meeting and conferring on:
- One financial-information custodian
- Confirmation of whether Mark Nappi is the appropriate NASA relationship custodian

### The Takeaway

ESI protocols are mechanisms to implement the Rules—not devices to contract around them. Relevance drives duty, not custodian designations.
